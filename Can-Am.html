Modelling and Control of an Off Road Vehicle

Despite recent progress in self-driving cars or autonomous features in advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS) to improve road safety, navigating through adverse conditions remains challenging, even at low speeds. When the vehicle is operating closer to the limit of its physical capabilities, such as moving at high velocities, on low grip surfaces such as snow or gravel, or attempting to avoid a collision, the challenge to navigate in a controlled manner is exacerbated. Unfortunately, under adverse conditions, the vehicle may be unstable and will need to operate beyond its physical limits to follow pre-specified routes. ADAS is designed to keep the vehicle operating well below its limits. Under extreme conditions, not only will it become a hinderance, but it may also compromise road safety. We aim to customize a Can-Am Maverick X3 into a reliable, self-driving research platform to develop and validate novel autonomous controllers that may improve road safety and aid further development in ADAS.
We elected a Can-Am as the base-platform due to its off-road capabilities, but it did not have decoded engine sensor data and lacked sensors such as steering position, brake pressure, and high-fidelity wheel speed sensors. Therefore, we started to work on embedding sensors and decoding engine sensor data, signal filtering, and sensor fusion, as well as automating the steering, throttle, and brakes using signal spoofing and/or actuators.
I am a member of the platform research team and I primary responsibilities entail designing and implementing the electrical systems and integrating them into the vehicle using mechanical means while factoring in features such as vibration and harshness, waterproofing, etc. I worked on both low-level electronics - high speed analog signal processing and filtering, and the main power distribution system - low voltage DC power electronics. I am also involved in communication and control archiecture design, as well as the hardware and embedded implementation of these features.
I joined the lab in October 2024 as a part-time undergraduate researcher, working up to 12 hrs/wk in the Fall 2024 semester, 20hrs/wk in the Spring 2025 semester, and full-time during Summer 2025 (funded by the Summer Undergraduate Research Award). I worked under Prof. Franck Djeumou, with Prof. Sandipan Mishra's guidance. I worked extensively with Lucas Geiss and Ruoxiang Zhao, two fellow undergraduate students at RPI, as well as Côme Vincent, a visiting scholar from l'École Polytechnique.
