Rensselaer Motorsport - Iterative Chassis Design Optimization

In chassis design, aside from accomodating the needs of all subsystems, the most important objective is lightweighting without compromising vehicle dynamics. As such, there are a few important metrics and targets - minimizing weight, maximizing torsional stiffness, maximize component and driver protection (particularly during impact and/or rollover), and minimize single failure points.
Since torsional stiffness have an inverse correlation with chassis weight, we set a minimum torsional stiffness target such that when combined with the suspension's contribution to body roll, the net vehicle roll gradient still meets the roll-stiffness targets.
Once we derived this target, we created an iterative beam FEA in ANSYS Mechanical, where all nodes are defined as points scraped as coordinates from CAD (in Solidworks) and various beam configurations are then created and simulated. There were multiple rounds of this simulation, the first focused on vehicle geometry rather than load accuracy. The second round introduced more beam profiles and enhanced the material properties used for deflection computation. The third round focused on introducing more suspension loads such as rocker loads- making the loading cases more realistic and focused on nodal deflection at specific points rather than overall chassis stiffness. There is a final round of study being worked on - focusing on introducing all suspension loads into the FEA, including bending loads from suspension arms, damper loads modeled as elastic loads on shell elements, and coupling MATLAB, ANSYS Mechanical, and Solidworks to iteratively optimize for the lightest possible chassis configuration.
Additionally, impact and failure studies following the same simulation mechanisms are also in progress.
