Student on the Beamline

It all started as a passion project over COVID, where a few friends and I found out that CERN has a student program called Beamline for Schools, where students get to propose experiment ideas and see it come to fruition at Deutsches Elektronen-Synchrotron (DESY), a Electron Sychrotron Facility in Germany. Our original proposal was named Targeting Cancer with Positron and Electron Irradiation Scattering Onset . We were interested in targetted radiation therapy for cancer. Specifically, we found that cancer cells has a negatively charged membrane while regular cells generally do not. Therefore, we proposed that the electrostatic attraction between positron and cancer cells may enhance the efficacy of targetted radiation therapy and minimize damage to surrounding tissues. We proposed to use Graphene Oxide (GO), approximately 1.1 ± 0.2 nm thick, to simulate cell membrane conditions, and measure the incident and scatter angles of 2 GeV positron and electron beams, to see if there exist a statistically-significant localization difference between the two beams (see proposed apparatus on the left).
We received an honorable mention and feedback on the feasibility of the proposal. Afterwards, we decided to pursue more local opportunities, still maintaining the goal of conducting an experiment in the field of bio-molecular interaction and at a synchrotron faclity. We found out that the Canadian Light Source (CLS), has a similar program called Students on the Beamline. We submitted a proposal and eventually were invited to perform the experiment on the Canadian Macromolecular Crystallography Facility, Bending Magnet Beamline (CMCF-BM).
X-ray crystallography remains one of the best imaging methods for biological materials due to its extremely high resolution. It allows for protein structure visualization at a molecular level, including indicatig the location of key metal ions, etc. However, due to exposure to high-energy x-ray beams for up to multiple seconds, protein may deform or even denature, reducing this technique's accuracy. We aimed to quantify the radiation damage of this method, particularly for bovine insulin by comparing our reconstructed files with its protein database (PDB) file. The results of this study was ultimately published at the 2024 CLS Annual User's Conference. Please see the slide deck below for additional information.
