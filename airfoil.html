Machine Learning Based Cost Reduction of Airfoil Optimization

Many marine, aerospace, and automotive products have highly complex geometry, wherefore prototyping for functional benchmarking is generally infeasible and analyzing their performance rely primarily on computational fluid dynamics (CFD) and other computer-aided engineering (CAE) software suites. Not only are they typically proprietary, design optimization, when coupled with high-order computational methods is highly costly. Through two different attempts, we aimed to take a step towards cost reduction of design optimization by reducing the need for iterative simulation using data augmentation and generative-design-inspried techniques. We chose airfoils as the target geometry because their 2D analysis data are easy to generate for a pilot study, but 3D analysis is sufficiently complicated such that it may benefit from a ML-based design optimization apparatus.
The first attempt entailed scraping data from airfoil databases such as the UIUC Airfoil Data Site and TU Delft's Airfoil Coordinate Database, as well as some published windtunnel data of DU-97 with various combinations of vortex generator geometries attached. The end goal was to produce an augmented database of airfoil and vortex generator combination for high-lift, low-drag applications. The algorithm performed well with validation data and was able to produce rough performance coefficients (Cl, Cd, etc) in just seconds (see the first document below for further results). However, due to the lack of experimental data on more airfoil-vortex-generator geometry combinations, the model lacked validation for low-thickness airfoil when vortex generators are attached.
The second attempt focused more on 2D airfoil data to remove the uncertainty in validation. It also allowed a higher-order airfoil geometry parametrization. The best training algorithm was found to be ExtraTrees, where it only took a few minutes to train on a standard desktop and was able to suggest multiple combination of airfoil geometrical characteristics based on the user-desired performance characteristics (see the second document below for further results).
I collaborated with Akira Yoshiyama on the second part of this project and we won multiple awards at the 2022 Fraser Valley Regional Science Fair and both the Gold Excellence Award (top 10 overall) and the NSERC Young Innovator's Award at the 2022 Canada-Wide Science Fair.
See the source code on our github repository: IntelligentAirfoilDesign
